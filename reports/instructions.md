# How to Complete the Team Status Report

Use `template.md` to prepare a report for each reporting period. Keep the report factual, concise, and focused on outcomes, risks, and decisions.

## 1. Complete the Header

- **Reporting period:** Use the start and end dates covered by the report.
- **Prepared by:** Add the person responsible for assembling the report.
- **Date prepared:** Use the date the report is published.
- **Overall status:** Select one RAG status:
  - **Green:** Work is on track and no major intervention is needed.
  - **Amber:** Work is progressing, but a risk or dependency needs attention.
  - **Red:** A major blocker or failure threatens the expected outcome or date.

## 2. Write the Executive Summary

Write 2-4 sentences for a reader who may only read this section. Include:

1. The most important outcome delivered.
2. Whether progress is on track.
3. The most significant risk or decision needed.

Avoid listing every task. Use plain language and include numbers or dates when they clarify the situation.

## 3. Record Progress

Add one row for each important workstream, goal, or deliverable.

- Use **Complete** when the outcome is finished and accepted.
- Use **In progress** when active work remains and the expected outcome is still achievable.
- Use **At risk** when a risk could affect scope, quality, or timing.
- Describe outcomes rather than activity. For example, write "released validation rules to production" instead of "worked on validation."
- Name the owner who can answer questions or coordinate the next step.

## 4. Add Metrics

Choose metrics that help the reader judge progress. Useful examples include:

- Work items completed.
- Sprint completion percentage.
- Defect count.
- Deployment success rate.
- Support volume.
- Hours remaining.
- Milestone completion.

For each metric, provide the current value, a target or comparison point, and a short interpretation. Do not include a number without explaining what it means.

## 5. Document Risks and Blockers

Add a row for each active concern.

- State the risk or blocker specifically.
- Rate **Impact** as Low, Medium, or High based on the consequence if it occurs or continues.
- Rate **Likelihood** as Low, Medium, or High based on the current evidence.
- Describe a concrete mitigation or next action.
- Assign one owner and a due date.
- Remove resolved risks from the next report, or note their resolution in the progress section.

A blocker should identify what is preventing progress. A risk should identify what might prevent progress.

## 6. Capture Decisions and Support Needed

Use this section when progress depends on a leadership decision, approval, staffing change, external dependency, or escalation.

State:

- What decision or support is needed.
- Who owns the decision.
- The date by which it is needed.
- What happens if the decision is delayed.

If no support is needed, write `None at this time.`

## 7. Set Next-Period Priorities

List the three most important priorities for the next period. Each priority should include:

- The action or deliverable.
- The expected outcome.
- The responsible owner.

Order the priorities by importance, urgency, or dependency.

## 8. Add Notes Carefully

Use Notes for useful context that does not fit elsewhere, such as links, assumptions, reporting limitations, or follow-up items. Do not hide risks or decisions in this section.

## Quality Checklist

Before publishing, confirm that:

- The overall status matches the evidence in the report.
- Every major progress item has an owner.
- Metrics include a target or comparison.
- Every active risk has a mitigation, owner, and due date.
- Decisions include a deadline and consequence of delay.
- The summary can be understood without opening another document.
- The report does not include confidential information that the audience should not see.
